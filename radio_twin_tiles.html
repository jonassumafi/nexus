Twin tile radio

The chosen layout, refined and stress tested. The window on the left is live and resizable. The scenarios on the right load each edge case, from an empty channel to a crowd of twenty-four, a dropped connection and a muted mic. The rules behind each one are in the table below.

Skin

CaseWhat the tile does
Empty channelChip area reads "No other operators" in muted text. The headcount reads 0 and is not clickable.
A few operatorsEvery username appears as a chip, newest first.
A crowdThe chip area wraps and never clips. It shows 3 chips under 400 pixels and 5 beyond, so the "+N" chip always stays inside two rows. A dashed "+N" chip covers the rest. The "+N" chip and the headcount both open the roster.
Who is talkingThe speaker moves to the front with a green outline and equalizer, and the count line shows their username. With two or more at once the radio goes into double talk. The tile border and display flash red, the line reads "Double talk". The app already plays its own double talk chirp, so the mockup stays silent.
Join and leaveA joiner flashes green with a plus for 2.4 seconds. A leaver turns red and is struck through for 2.4 seconds, then drops off. Both move to the front while they flash. Each event also writes to the activity strip and to a screen reader announcement.
BurstsEvents of the same kind on the same radio within 4 seconds merge into one row, such as "Wen Li and 4 others joined". The strip keeps two rows and dims any older than 8 seconds.
Long usernamesAny name over 12 characters is cut to about half its length with an ellipsis, in chips, the roster and the strip. The full name shows as a tooltip.
RosterOne roster is open at a time, in a full-width panel under the tiles. It scrolls after about six rows, and closes with the close button, the Escape key or a second click on the headcount.
Locked frequencyClicking the display shakes it and shows a short note. The lock must be released before typing.
TuningClicking an unlocked display opens a text field. Enter applies a value between 50.000 and 350.000. Escape or clicking away cancels. A bad value shakes the field and keeps the old frequency.
DisconnectedThe tile dims, the transmit button disables, and a Reconnect button replaces the chips. The power button turns red.
Connection lostAn amber pulsing dot and a "Reconnecting" line replace the headcount. The names dim as last known, transmit disables, and the radio retries on its own. The roster header says "Last known list."
Mic mutedBoth transmit buttons read "Mic muted" and disable. Pressing a transmit key shows a short note.
Narrow windowUnder 300 pixels the tiles stack in one column, Crew above Command, and the window grows taller instead of cutting content.
Reduced motionAll flashes, strikes and equalizers become static. Plus and minus marks on chips still show who joined or left.
